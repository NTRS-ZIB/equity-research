D2

The PUCT must amend its transmission cost-allocation rules

Down
Timing By December 31, 2026
Impact Medium
Confidence High
Provenance Filed

Senate Bill 6 requires the PUCT to amend its wholesale transmission cost-allocation rules by December 31, 2026, and the company states the Commission is considering measures that could require large loads to bear a greater share of transmission system upgrade costs, to pay minimum demand charges based on contracted peak demand and to move from the current four coincident peak methodology to a twelve coincident peak methodology, any of which could increase our or our tenants' transmission-related costs.

This one reaches the signed leases, which is what separates it from D1. A transmission charge falls on an operating load, not on a queue position. The three leases are triple-net and the company recovers certain operating expenses from tenants at cost, so the first-order incidence is the tenant's; but a cost increase at a site a tenant has not yet taken delivery of is a renegotiation risk rather than a pass-through.

The rules implementing SB 6's large-load interconnection standards were adopted in September 2026 and became effective on October 8, 2026, so this is the second of two deadlines rather than the first.

Upside

A cost-allocation rule the company can plan against removes an open variable, and a 12CP methodology rewards a load that can curtail. The company has committed to curtailing during ERCOT grid emergencies and is building behind-the-meter generation, which is the hedge.

Downside

Minimum demand charges based on contracted peak demand would be payable on capacity whether or not a tenant is drawing it, which is exactly the position at a site mid-delivery. Neither the company nor this document has sized it.

Analyst assumption

No quantification of any of these measures is available, from the issuer or from here. The company lists them as things the Commission is considering. The deadline is statutory and the direction of travel is one way, which is why this is marked Down rather than two-sided, but the magnitude is not established and is not estimated.
