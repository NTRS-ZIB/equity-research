Basis

  1. Confidence rates the date, never the outcome. A catalyst can arrive exactly when this calendar said and deliver the thing it warned about: the CLARITY Act Senate floor vote (R14) was carried at medium to high confidence about 15 September 2026, the vote was held that day, and cloture was rejected 49 to 50. The date was right and the outcome was the downside case.
  2. Standing conditions are exposures without an event date. They take “Why undated” in place of confidence, because rating a continuously traded price or an ongoing policy for timing certainty would say nothing. Undated does not mean unimportant: the first tenant lease is the highest-impact item here and it sits in the standing class.
  3. Horizon bands divide near from far, read against the as-of in the masthead. This document states no absolute boundary dates for them: a boundary written down is falsified by the next restamp while the convention behind it is not, and the previous revision's boundaries had all three bands anchored to a month that is now in the past. Where the company has guided a range rather than a date, the item sits in the band containing the midpoint and the range is stated in Timing.
  4. An ID marks position in the current ordering and changes between revisions, so catalysts are named by title with the ID in parentheses.
