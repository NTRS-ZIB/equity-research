02Business model

Riot Platforms owns and operates large-scale power infrastructure in Texas and Kentucky and monetizes it three ways. It mines bitcoin for its own account, taking the coin as revenue. It leases developed data center capacity to third parties under long-term contracts. And it sells electrical engineering, switchgear and power distribution equipment through two acquired businesses, ESS Metron and E4A Solutions. The company reports these as three segments: Bitcoin Mining, Data Center and Engineering.

The unifying asset is interconnected power with the approvals already in hand. Developed power capacity was 1,292 MW at June 30, 2026, across Rockdale at 700 MW, Corsicana at 400 MW and Kentucky at 192 MW. What has changed in 2026 is not the asset but the customer: capacity that used to be consumed internally by miners is now being let to tenants under contracts measured in decades rather than in blocks.

The payment mechanics differ sharply between the three. Mining revenue is received in bitcoin at the moment it is earned, so it is non-cash on receipt and its value moves before it can be realized. Data center revenue is contractual rent, billed and paid in dollars, and it does not begin until capacity is delivered. Engineering revenue is project work recognized over time, and it carries contract assets of $19.9 million at June 30, 2026, of which $12.5 million is engineering and $7.4 million tenant fit-out.

Analyst assumption

The characterization of Riot as a power business with three monetization routes rather than as a bitcoin miner that also leases space is a reading, not a company statement. It is taken from the segment note and the capacity disclosures: the same 700 MW at Rockdale supports the mining fleet, the AMD lease and the 191 MW tenant lease, and the company's own stated aim is to reduce direct exposure to cryptocurrency price movements by broadening into digital infrastructure.

Total revenue rose 13.9% while the largest stream fell 19%. That is the whole of the business story in one line. Bitcoin Mining lost $27.2 million of quarterly revenue year on year and the two other segments added $48.4 million between them. On a six-month basis the same pattern holds: mining fell from $283.7 million to $225.6 million, Data Center contributed $56.4 million from nothing, and Engineering more than doubled from $24.5 million to $59.5 million.

The mining decline is not a volume failure. Quantity of bitcoin mined rose to 1,587 coins in the quarter from 1,426, and deployed hash rate rose to 44.4 EH/s from 35.4 EH/s. What fell was the value of each coin produced: $71,667 against $98,800. The company mined more and was paid less for it, which is the network economics rather than an operating problem.

Data Center revenue at $23.2 million in the quarter comes entirely from the AMD lease, since the 191 MW tenant lease was signed after the quarter ended and delivers nothing before December 2027. The forward step from $23.2 million a quarter to a lease expected to yield approximately $9.1 billion over twenty years is therefore not a growth rate to extrapolate; it is a different contract that starts in 2028.

04Industry & market backdrop

Two markets bear on this company and they are moving in opposite directions.

Bitcoin mining economics compressed over the year to June 2026. The production value of one bitcoin mined fell 27.5%, from $98,800 to $71,667, while the cost of power for self-mining rose from $62.2 million to $73.5 million in the quarter. Riot's combined all-in power cost was 3.6 cents per kilowatt hour in the quarter against 3.5 a year earlier. A miner with a low power cost stays profitable across a wider band of bitcoin prices, and Riot's position on that curve is what has allowed it to keep expanding hash rate while the value of each coin produced fell.

Demand for large blocks of near-term interconnected power is the opposite story. The scarce input is not land or capital but energized capacity with grid approvals already granted, because a project still in an interconnection queue cannot commit to a delivery date. Riot has leased 241 MW of critical IT load at Rockdale inside roughly seven months, 50 MW to Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. with an option to expand to 200 MW, and 191 MW to an unnamed counterparty. That is the market repricing capacity that already exists.

Texas is the venue for both, and its rules are being rewritten. The company's own risk disclosure states that Senate Bill 6 requires the Public Utility Commission of Texas to amend wholesale transmission cost-allocation rules by December 31, 2026, and that the Commission is considering measures requiring large loads to bear a greater share of transmission upgrade costs, to pay minimum demand charges on contracted peak demand for twenty years, and to move away from the four coincident peak allocation method. It also states these actions could delay or prevent interconnection of projects still advancing through the ERCOT process.

Analyst assumption

Increased scrutiny of new large loads plausibly raises the value of capacity that is already approved and energized, which would favor an incumbent like Riot over a developer still in the queue. That is an inference from the direction of the rulemaking rather than anything the Commission has decided, and it cuts the other way on cost: the same rules that slow a competitor's project also raise what Riot pays for transmission on the load it already has. Power curtailment credits of $31.1 million in the half are the specific figure at risk if the four coincident peak method changes.

Total costs and expenses were negative $63.2 million in the second quarter of 2025. That is not a misprint and it is the single most misleading number in this company's accounts. Bitcoin fair-value movement runs through costs, so a quarter in which the price rises can produce a negative cost line and an operating profit without a single operational change.

The consequence is that the swing from $216.1 million of operating income to a $239.4 million operating loss is largely a mark rather than a deterioration in trading. Read as a collapse in the business it is wrong; read as unimportant it is also wrong, because the same mechanism sets the carrying value of the treasury that secures the Coinbase facility. The figure to watch instead is gross revenue by segment and the cost of power, neither of which contains a mark.

The cash statements say something the income statement does not. In the six months to June 30, 2026 operating activities used $272.8 million, investing activities provided $514.8 million, and financing activities used $2.9 million. The investing inflow is principally $131.8 million from selling bitcoin and $14.7 million from marketable securities, against $93.2 million spent on property and equipment and $86.4 million on miners. Cash and restricted cash rose to $548.9 million from $309.8 million.

So the company funded a $272.8 million operating outflow and roughly $180 million of capital spending by selling assets, not by raising money. No cash was raised through the issuance of common stock or debt in the period. Whether that is prudence or necessity is not settled by the filings; what is settled is that the treasury is the funding source and it is finite.

The equity rollforward shows the same movement inside the quarter: 378,978,341 shares at April 1, 2026, less 955,377 for issuance of restricted stock net of forfeitures and delivery of shares underlying awards net of tax withholding, giving 378,022,964 at June 30. Weighted average shares went 175,026,051 in 2023, 275,980,010 in 2024 and 340,719,298 in 2025, an increase of 95% in two years. Set against that, no cash at all was raised from stock or debt in the first half of 2026 and the $500.0 million program stands untouched. The recent restraint is real and it is six months old; the three-year record is the base rate.

09Growth drivers

Ranked by how much of the current enterprise value each would have to justify.

1. Delivery of the 191 MW tenant lease. Approximately $9.1 billion of base rent over twenty years from full deployment in June 2028, with two five-year renewals at the lessee's option. Phased delivery of 96 MW in December 2027 and the balance in June 2028. This is the driver: nothing else on this list is of comparable size, and nothing of it reaches revenue for almost two years.

2. Expansion of the AMD lease. The existing agreement covers 50 MW of critical IT load with the potential for expansion up to a total of 200 MW at Rockdale. The expansion is an option and not a commitment, and the filings state no contracted value for either the base lease or the expansion. What it has already demonstrated is that the capacity is lettable to a named, investment-grade counterparty, which is a fact the unnamed tenant lease does not supply.

3. The remaining uncommitted capacity. Developed power capacity is 1,292 MW; the two Rockdale leases account for 241 MW of critical IT load. Critical IT load and developed power are not the same measure, since the second includes the overhead the first consumes, so the residual cannot be computed from these two figures alone. What can be said is that a substantial majority of developed capacity is not under a data center lease and is currently used for mining or is undeployed.

4. The engineering business. Revenue rose from $10.6 million to $37.3 million in the quarter and from $24.5 million to $59.5 million in the half. It is the fastest-growing segment in percentage terms and the one least discussed, and it has a strategic function beyond its revenue: ESS Metron and E4A Solutions supply the switchgear and power distribution that the Rockdale build needs, at a moment when equipment lead times are the binding constraint on delivery.

5. Mining efficiency. Hash rate rose to 44.4 EH/s from 35.4 EH/s year on year and Kentucky's developed capacity nearly tripled to 192 MW from 65 MW. This grows the smallest multiple rather than the largest: mining is 65.3% of current revenue and the segment whose revenue per unit of output fell 27.5% over the year.

10Risks

Severity-ranked, most severe first.

The interim facility falls due before permanent financing exists Severe

The $573.0 million facility matures on October 15, 2026 per the quarterly report or December 31, 2026 per the current report, and no permanent facility, commitment letter, backstop or equity issue appears in any filing. It is non-recourse to Riot Platforms, Inc. beyond customary carve-outs, so a failure lands on the project subsidiary and its collateral rather than on the parent's balance sheet. The cost is not a parent default; it is the loss of the equipment and the delivery schedule that the $9.1 billion lease depends on.

The tenant is not identified and its credit cannot be assessed Severe

The lease counterparty is described only as a leading frontier AI lab. No rating, guarantee, letter of credit, deposit or prepayment is disclosed. A twenty-year obligation of approximately $9.1 billion is the largest single item in the investment case and no public document allows a reader to judge whether it will be paid. The trigger would be a termination or renegotiation disclosure; the cost would be most of the reason the shares are priced where they are.

The treasury is being consumed and over half of what remains is pledged Severe

Holdings fell from 18,005 bitcoin to 11,380 in six months, and 5,821 of those are pledged to Coinbase Credit, Inc., leaving 5,559 free at a fair value of $325.4 million. Asset sales funded a $272.8 million operating outflow and roughly $180 million of capital spending in the half. The reserve is finite and the encumbered portion cannot be sold. If the build consumes the free block before lease revenue starts in 2028, the remaining sources are the untouched $500.0 million equity program or new debt.

Bitcoin price moves both the revenue line and the balance sheet at once Moderate

Fair-value movement runs through costs, so the reported operating result swings by hundreds of millions on the mark: total costs were negative $63.2 million in the second quarter of 2025 and positive $413.7 million in the same quarter of 2026. The same price sets the value of the collateral behind the Coinbase facility and the value of each coin mined, which fell from $98,800 to $71,667 year on year. Graded moderate rather than severe because the data center business is contracted in dollars and is the part of the company the valuation now rests on.

Texas may reprice what a large load pays for transmission Moderate

The company discloses that Senate Bill 6 requires the Public Utility Commission of Texas to amend wholesale transmission cost-allocation rules by December 31, 2026, and that measures under consideration would make large loads bear more of the transmission upgrade cost, pay minimum demand charges on contracted peak for twenty years, and lose the four coincident peak method. Power curtailment credits were $31.1 million in the half and are the specific figure at risk. It also discloses that these actions could delay or prevent interconnection of projects still in the ERCOT queue.

Dilution capacity is large, registered and immediately available Moderate

72,504,968 shares were potentially dilutive at June 30, 2026, of which 39,988,127 sit under the 2030 Notes, which became convertible at holders' option in the third quarter and which the company states it can and intends to settle in stock. A further $500.0 million of registered equity capacity, roughly 7% of the capitalization, can be sold without advance notice. Weighted average shares rose 95% over the two years to 2025. Graded moderate because none of it was used in the first half of 2026.

Governance and audit tenure Low

The board remains classified until the 2029 annual meeting under the phased declassification adopted in March 2026, nine months after the advisory vote that prompted it, so directors elected in 2026 and 2027 sit for three years. Deloitte & Touche LLP has served as auditor since 2023, a short tenure over a business that has changed shape twice in that period. Neither is a live problem in the filed record; both lengthen the interval before one would surface.

11Bull / base / bear

Each case with its preconditions: what must be true, not what might be.

Bear

The bridge does not term out on acceptable terms. Financing the build then falls to the treasury and the equity program, at which point the free 5,559 bitcoin and $500.0 million of registered stock are consumed against a lease that pays nothing before June 2028. Mining keeps shrinking, the value per coin mined keeps falling, and the enterprise value proves to have been an option on a counterparty nobody could assess.

Requires: no permanent facility by either stated maturity; continued treasury drawdown; and either an ATM drawdown or a delivery slip past June 2028.

Base

The interim facility is refinanced, the phased delivery holds to December 2027 and June 2028, and the Data Center segment grows from $23.2 million a quarter on the AMD lease while mining declines gradually. Nothing of the $9.1 billion reaches revenue inside the coverage window, so the shares trade on delivery milestones and financing news rather than on reported results.

Requires: a refinancing filed before December 31, 2026; both delivery dates held; and no ATM issuance beyond what the build needs.

Bull

Permanent financing closes ring-fenced at the project, the tenant is named or credit support is filed, and the AMD option is exercised toward its 200 MW ceiling. Riot is then an operator of interconnected Texas capacity with two decades of dollar-denominated contracted rent, holding the residual of 1,292 MW of developed power plus an in-house equipment supplier at exactly the moment lead times bind.

Requires: a non-recourse takeout; identification or credit support for the tenant; and evidence that uncommitted capacity can be let on comparable terms.

12Research summary

Riot has converted power into contracted revenue faster than any figure in its accounts yet shows. Inside roughly seven months it let 241 MW of critical IT load at Rockdale across two counterparties, and the larger of the two leases carries approximately $9.1 billion of base rent over twenty years against total 2025 revenue of $647.4 million. The Data Center segment did not exist a year ago and produced $23.2 million in the second quarter.

None of that is in the reported numbers, and will not be until 2028. The lease term begins on full deployment anticipated in June 2028, with the first 96 MW in December 2027. Until then the company reports a shrinking mining business, a fast-growing engineering business, one small lease, and a loss driven substantially by the bitcoin mark: $737.6 million for the first half, of which most is the fair-value mechanism rather than trading.

The gap between those two facts is a financing problem, and it is the whole of the near term. The only committed money is a $573.0 million interim facility at a project subsidiary which the company itself dates two ways, October 15 or December 31 of this year. Everything beyond it is absent from the record: no permanent facility, no backstop, no commitment. Meanwhile the treasury that has been funding operations fell 37% in six months and over half of what remains is pledged.

Filing status

Riot Platforms has filed fifteen late-filing notifications across the 1,592 filings on its index: eight on Form NT 10-K and seven on Form NT 10-Q, the earliest on November 15, 2002 and the most recent on March 2, 2023. Nine fall after October 19, 2017 and six belong to the earlier company. None has been filed in the three years and five months since. The whole index was searched on August 16, 2026 across both of its pages, the recent page holding 1,005 filings back to January 25, 2013 and an overflow page holding 587 more back to February 7, 2002.

That pagination is why the count is stated with the index size behind it. A search of the recent page alone would have covered 63% of the record, missed six of the fifteen notifications, and produced a figure that looked complete.

An earlier identity, and the date it ended

The Central Index Key long predates the business. Filings begin on February 7, 2002, more than a decade before any bitcoin operation, because the registrant was incorporated in Colorado on July 24, 2000 under the name AspenBio, Inc. The company states the transition in terms: "Effective October 19, 2017, we adopted the corporate name Riot Blockchain, Inc., and changed our state of incorporation to Nevada." It adopted its current name on December 30, 2022 and remains a Nevada corporation.

Two things follow that a reader would otherwise get wrong. An eighteen-filing history of annual reports is not eighteen years of this company: it is roughly nine years of this one and fifteen of a diagnostics business that happened to keep the same registration. And the state of incorporation changed with the identity rather than separately from it, so every filing before October 19, 2017 was made by a Colorado corporation and every one after it by a Nevada one.
