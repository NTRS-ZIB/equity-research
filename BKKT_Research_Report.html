02Business model

Bakkt sells infrastructure, not consumer products. It does not compete for retail accounts against Coinbase or Robinhood. It supplies the licensed, regulated back end that other firms integrate in order to offer crypto trading or stablecoin payments to their own users under their own brand.

The company was founded in 2018 as a subsidiary of Intercontinental Exchange and went public via a SPAC merger in October 2021. That heritage is the source of its principal asset: the licensing stack. Its subsidiary holds a New York BitLicense, money transmitter licenses across US states where required, and is registered with FinCEN as a money services business.

Three stated growth engines

Since the January 2026 rebrand from Bakkt Holdings, Inc. to Bakkt, Inc., management organizes the business around three pillars:

  • Bakkt Markets. The legacy business. APIs and an embedded web experience through which partner firms offer buying, selling and holding of around 45 digital assets. Essentially all current revenue originates here: of the $147.5M of Total Transacting Volume in Q3 2026, $141.7M is digital asset trading and the company says trading activity on Bakkt Markets declined from the second quarter of 2026 and remained below the levels experienced in the corresponding periods of the prior year. The asset count is not restated in the Q3 report.
  • Bakkt Agent. No longer unlaunched, and the filing settles the disagreement this report had carried. The Form 10-Q of November 9, 2026 reports 9,760 Monthly Active Users for September 2026 and says September is the first period for which the measure is available from our reporting systems, so fiat was moving across Bakkt-operated rails inside the guided quarter. It also splits the product exactly as the August slide did: Embedded Finance modules are available and partners are integrating them, while the co-branded credit card programs, Neobank-as-a-Service and the stablecoin card-as-a-service program are planned and remain in development. So one of three paths shipped and two did not, which vindicates the slide on the first and this report on the rest.

    The description also changed, and in a direction worth naming. The 10-Q now calls Agent our AI-native financial services layer, which takes the regulated capabilities delivered through Bakkt Markets' APIs and puts an intelligent agent in control of them, using AI to interpret intent, orchestrate multi-step financial actions, and execute transactions on a client's or customer's behalf. The earlier framing was programmatic onboarding and money movement. The capability described is the same stack; the category it is sold into is not.

    What it still does not do is produce disclosed revenue. No partner is named, and revenue is not disaggregated by engine. The metric is also narrower than it sounds: a user transacting through more than one client application is counted once for each, because our platforms do not share a common end-user identifier, so such users cannot currently be deduplicated across applications, making 9,760 a ceiling on unique individuals. Against the 25,000 year-end target the August presentation printed, September is 39% of the way there. Estimate
  • Bakkt Global. Not an operating business but an investment portfolio: roughly 28% of Bitcoin Japan Corporation, Tokyo-listed, and a warrant position in Transchem Limited, BSE-listed, in India. It is now also the largest thing on the balance sheet. The Transchem warrants are carried at $134.8M at September 30, 2026 and the Japan stake at $10.4M, so Strategic Asset Value of $145.2M exceeds the whole market capitalisation of about $124.1M. Estimate In India the company says Transchem is pursuing broker-led distribution through acquisitions of, and partnerships with, local brokers; in Japan it says the position provides supply and market access, which during the third quarter of 2026 extended to Japan's private AI and robotics ecosystem, which is not explained further.

Bakkt Global is a holding company activity rather than a service anyone buys, and the Strategic Asset Value KPI reports the mark on an investment portfolio rather than operating performance. One of the three pillars now has a user metric, one has essentially all the revenue, and the third has most of the assets. That is a different shape from the one this report described in September, when two of three were pre-revenue or non-operating, and it is not obviously a better one: the engine carrying the balance sheet is the one that sells nothing.

The digital asset treasury strategy underneath

Recent filings carry extensive language about an updated investment policy and related treasury strategy, including risk factors on digital asset price volatility, fair-value accounting of holdings, exposure to the corporate alternative minimum tax on unrealized gains, and competition by other Bitcoin treasury companies. The Q3 report sizes none of it. Strategic Asset Value as the company now defines it contains two foreign equity positions and no digital assets at all, and no Bitcoin or stablecoin holding of the company's own is quantified anywhere in the filing. This report named the Q3 print as what would confirm or refute the structure described below; that remedy has now been spent without result. Board appointments align: Mike Alfred, a director of IREN and Eaglebrook Advisors, and Lyn Alden, a Swan Bitcoin director and general partner at Ego Death Capital, joined in autumn 2025.

If accurate, that reframes Bakkt Global from a geographic diversification program into an indirect offshore vehicle for Bitcoin exposure, which would mean the Strategic Asset Value KPI carries more crypto beta than a portfolio of foreign financial stakes implies. This structure has not been confirmed from the 10-Q, and the source is a secondary account of a director's remarks rather than a company disclosure. 04Industry & market backdrop

Stablecoin float and stablecoin throughput have diverged

As of mid-2026 the sector has split, and the split governs how the backdrop reads for a transaction-fee business.

The sector figures below are stamped at late July 2026 and were not re-measured for this revision, because no market data source available to this document answers a client that identifies itself honestly. What has been added is the issuer's own filed account of the regulatory and competitive developments since, which follows them. Float is contracting. Total stablecoin market capitalization fell to roughly $310B by late July 2026, down more than $10B from its May peak, the largest monthly decline since the Terra collapse in May 2022. One contributing mechanism is the GENIUS Act prohibition on issuers paying interest on payment stablecoins, which has moved capital toward yield-bearing alternatives.

Throughput is rising. Over the same period, adjusted stablecoin transaction volume reached a record $1.79 trillion in June 2026, up 63% month over month.

The second figure is the one that bears on Bakkt, which earns a spread on transactions rather than a yield on float. A contracting float with rising throughput is a more favorable configuration for a transaction-fee business than the reverse, though it also describes a sector losing capital even as its rails are used harder.

Estimates of real-economy stablecoin payment volume put 2025 between $350B and $550B, growing around 60% year over year, with business-to-business settlement roughly 60% of the total.

Regulation cuts both ways

The rulemaking has moved and the issuer's Form 10-Q is the record used here. Per that filing: the GENIUS Act was enacted in July 2025 and effective January 18, 2027; the Treasury proposed rules governing the issuance, offer and sale of payment stablecoins, including a prohibition from July 18, 2028 on digital asset service providers offering stablecoins not issued by a permitted issuer; the Federal Reserve proposed rules for issuers it supervises; the Stablecoin Certification Review Committee issued interim procedures; and the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency issued its final rule for issuers under its jurisdiction, with several comment periods still open. Market structure legislation did not advance: the U.S. Senate did not invoke cloture on the CLARITY Act. The MiCA transitional period ended on July 1, 2026 and European crypto-asset service providers now require MiCA authorisation, while the United Kingdom's regime takes effect in October 2027. The GENIUS Act established a federal framework for payment stablecoins, and MiCA is in hard enforcement. For a licensed operator this is a tailwind: regulated counterparties can transact without legal ambiguity. The same clarity invites banks in, and that is no longer commentary. The Form 10-Q records that A group of 21 banks and financial institutions, including Bank of America, Citi, Goldman Sachs and Wells Fargo, announced plans to establish a company to issue a U.S. dollar stablecoin compliant with the GENIUS Act and MiCA, with a market launch targeted for the first half of 2027. In the same passage the OCC granted final approval for Circle to open a national trust bank. A consortium with a date is a different fact from a prediction that banks are well placed.

Cross-border payments is the stated target

Management cites a $44 trillion global payments market. Independent sizing of the stablecoin cross-border opportunity is smaller: one estimate puts the base addressable market at $17.9 trillion.

Addressable market size does not indicate capture. Retained revenue last quarter was $1.66M, and the payments and stablecoin conversion activity that would address this market was $5.8M of the quarter's $147.5M of transacting volume, which is 3.9%. The distance between a $44 trillion market and $1.66M retained is where the risk sits, and market-size figures in company materials are context rather than evidence. One comparable figure from the same filing sets the scale: the company records that in October 2026 Visa reported that its stablecoin settlement volume exceeded a $25 billion annualized run rate, which is about 42 times Bakkt's annualised Q3 volume. Analyst assumption

The Q3 2026 10-Q carries a going concern discussion and management concluded that cash will be sufficient to fund Bakkt's operations for 12 months from the date these financial statements are issued. That is not a substantial doubt statement. What the conclusion rests on has changed, and the change is material.

The forecast still excludes revenue from products not yet live, which this report has carried since the first quarter: cash flow forecasts exclude anticipated revenue from prospective products not yet operational on the Bakkt platform. It now also includes something. The forecasts are based on projected transaction volumes for existing live products, pipeline opportunities, products in which the infrastructure has been completed, and the receipt of approximately $17 million expected proceeds from our pending litigation with Roman, and may be further supplemented, as needed, by anticipated proceeds from the sale of existing investments.

That litigation is not final. Bakkt is seeking approximately $10.0 million and attorneys' fees from Project Labrador Holdco, which bought the loyalty business; Roman has counterclaimed for $19 million. Roman filed a notice of appeal on August 6, 2026 and subsequently withdrew its notice of appeal upon being informed that the Superior Court's judgment is not final until the fee motion is resolved, and may re-file the appeal once the judgment is final. Briefing on the fee motion is underway. So a twelve-month liquidity conclusion on a $37.3M balance counts roughly $17M that depends on a judgment the counterparty may still appeal, and the gap between the $10.0M claimed and the $17M forecast is not explained in the filing.

12Research summary

The question that resolves the name

The question has changed, and that is the finding. It was whether Bakkt could grow transacting volume roughly tenfold, or materially raise the 0.68% take rate, before dilution and cash burn consumed the equity value that exists today. It raised the take rate, to 1.12%, and cut the cost base by $4.4M in a quarter, which cut required breakeven volume from about $17B a year to about $5.7B. Can Bakkt fund itself to the point where the improving spread meets a volume base that is still shrinking?

Everything else is downstream of that. $37.3M of cash against a $17.9M quarterly burn is about two quarters; the twelve-month going-concern conclusion counts roughly $17M from a lawsuit whose judgment is not final; $27.8M may be called for India; and a meaningful portion of available cash is committed to counterparty credit in institutional trading. The $300M at-the-market program is the answer to all of it and is 2.4 times the market capitalisation, so using it is the dilution the first question was about, arriving through the cash door rather than the growth door.

Where the company stands

Bakkt is an early-stage business inside a formerly larger company's shell. Stripped of the loyalty division, the trust company and its dominant client, what remains is a licensed crypto and stablecoin infrastructure provider that retained $1.66M of revenue last quarter against $16.1M of controllable costs, holding $37.3M of cash with no long-term debt and $145.2M of foreign strategic positions it cannot readily realise.

Improved over the quarter, and these are real: the take rate doubled; controllable costs fell 21%; retained revenue rose year over year for the first time in this report's history; the Bakkt Agent rails went live and reported a user metric; the securities class action was dismissed with the appeal waived; the public warrant overhang expired to nothing; the at-the-market program went a sixth month untouched; and Strategic Asset Value was redefined onto the audited accounts, which closes a measurement complaint this report had carried since July.
