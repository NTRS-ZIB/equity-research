Basis

  1. Timing confidence rates the date, never the outcome. A High-confidence catalyst can still be a coin flip: the Q2 2026 results (D1) are near-certain to occur and could contain very poor numbers.
  2. An item with no date is a standing condition rather than a dated catalyst, however material it is. Standing conditions take “Why undated” in place of timing confidence, because a scale that measures timing cannot rate something that has none.
  3. Impact is judged against a market capitalisation near $320M, not in absolute dollars. A $28M cash call is High impact here and would be immaterial at a larger company.
  4. These items are correlated, not independent. The gating chain below states the dependency, and the standing conditions load on shared crypto and liquidity factors.

Banded 0–3 / 3–6 / 6–12 months, earliest first. Numbering is gapless and runs straight through the bands, so a boundary never resets the count. An ID marks position in the current ordering and changes between revisions, so catalysts are named by title.

The gating chain

These are not thirteen independent shots on goal. A failure early in the sequence invalidates what sits below it: the Bakkt Agent launch (D3) gates partner activation, which gates volume against the year-end Total Transacting Volume target (D7), which gates retained revenue at the Q3 2026 results (D4) and the FY2026 results (D10), which is what would make equity issuance optional rather than necessary. Separately, cash is contested: the remaining Transchem warrant exercise of roughly $28M (D13) competes with operating runway.

GENIUS Act final rules publication window

Two-sided
Timing Late 2026 to early 2027
Impact Medium
Confidence Low
Provenance Market

The statutory rulemaking deadline of 18 July 2026 passed with no final rules published. Open comment periods extend past it: the FDIC compliance proposal closes 4 August 2026 and a five-agency customer identification proposal closes 21 August 2026. Equity issuance overhang and the active ATM

Two-sided
Timing Continuous
Impact High

Provenance Filed

Bakkt funds itself with equity. The ATM established under a Sales Agreement dated 16 January 2026, announced 20 January, carries capacity of up to $300M of common stock through seven sales agents, and remains available under an effective shelf at management's discretion, disclosed only after the fact. Against a market capitalisation near $320M the authorised capacity is close to the whole company. Usage to 31 March 2026 was 1,990,434 shares for $21.5M gross and $20.8M net. Separately and larger, a resale registration filed 12 May 2026 and declared effective 28 May 2026, with its prospectus filed 27 May, covers up to 21,010,640 shares, approximately 43.9% of outstanding voting securities, held by insiders including the CEO at 8,926,320 and ICE at 8,380,362. S5

Related-party governance and key-person concentration

Two-sided
Timing Standing
Impact High
Why undated A feature of the structure, not an event
Provenance Filed

Akshay Naheta is CEO, President, a board member, and the chief operating decision maker for segment reporting. He sold DTR, which he owned, to the company for roughly 31.5% of it, and had extended DTR a €1.45M interest-free shareholder loan. He sits on the board of Bitcoin Japan Corporation, in which Bakkt holds roughly 28%. Three weeks after the DTR close, Bakkt acquired Gyzer Inc. from Daniel Ishag, its own Chief Commercial Officer, for 94,595 shares plus warrants over 250,000. Five of six directors joined within three years, and the company has used three auditors in three years: Ernst & Young for FY2023, KPMG for FY2024, Grant Thornton for FY2025.

Process protections were real and disclosed for DTR: a Special Committee of independent directors approved it, Naheta recused himself, and shareholders voted on 17 April 2026. Upside

Alignment, and more than rhetorical. A CEO whose performance units sit 62% out of the money is paid only if the shares regain the $18.66 hurdle, and has little near-term reason to sell into the resale registration.

Downside

A third related-party transaction would be hard to characterise as opportunistic. Key-person risk is acute: a departure would remove the author of the entire strategy, and the main contractual protection is a non-competition agreement running to the first anniversary of the DTR closing, extendable to the second.
